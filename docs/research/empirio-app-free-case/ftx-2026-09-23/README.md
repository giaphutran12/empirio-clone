## Narration, merged from the two videos

Edward pasted both transcripts with the ending duplicated at the top. "Finance" in the dictation is Binance.

**Video 1, before the decision:**

He raised $1.8 billion from Sequoia, SoftBank, and Tiger Global. He testified before Congress. He was worth $16 billion on paper. Then it all vanished in 6 days.

November 2022, Nassau, Bahamas. Sam Bankman-Fried runs FTX from a beachside penthouse, a crypto exchange valued at $32 billion, eight times Coinbase, the golden boy of digital finance. On November 2, CoinDesk publishes a single document: Alameda Research's balance sheet. It shows $5.8 billion of its $14.6 billion in assets held as FTT, a token FTX itself created, collateral printed from thin air.

Customers start reading; questions multiply. FTT's price begins to slide. SBF tweets that everything is fine, assets are fine, the exchange is solvent, but the math is right there. The queue to withdraw is forming. Do you trust him or do you run?

**Video 2, after the decision:**

Confirmation: they hit withdraw and ran. Within 72 hours, customers pulled $6 billion from FTX. The queue moved fast at first, then it stopped. Behind them, millions of other users refreshed their screens to frozen balances and error messages.

SBF went on Twitter. He promised liquidity. He promised solvency. He announced a rescue deal with Binance, then Binance walked away after one look at the books. No independent auditor had ever reviewed those books. The Bahamas address wasn't an accident. It was a moat against scrutiny.

November 11, 9 days after the CoinDesk article, FTX filed for bankruptcy. $8 billion in customer funds gone. John Ray III, the man who unwound Enron, called FTX the worst corporate failure he had ever seen. SBF was arrested in December, sentenced to 25 years in March 2024.

The signal was always there. The balance sheet told the truth before anyone was ready to believe it. Fraud doesn't announce itself. It just waits for someone to do the math.

## Case copy, verbatim

Perspective options:
- Follow the smart money. If Sequoia and SoftBank are in, the due diligence has been done. Trust the consensus.
- Dig into the numbers first. Extraordinary returns demand extraordinary proof. Read the balance sheet before believing.
- Take a small bet, stay watchful. Participate enough to not miss out, but keep exposure low until the mechanics are clear.

Decision options:
- Pull everything out now. The balance sheet math doesn't work. Get liquid before the queue closes.
- Hold, trust the brand. SBF has congressional credibility and tier-1 backers. One bad article isn't the whole story.
- Withdraw half, wait on the rest. Hedge the uncertainty, reduce exposure without a full panic exit.

Analysis for "withdraw half": Those who moved slowly recovered only a fraction, withdrawals were frozen within 72 hours of the CoinDesk report. *Hedging felt rational, but this wasn't a volatile market, it was a collapsing fraud. When the underlying structure is rotten, partial caution isn't a hedge. The withdrawal window closed faster than a halfway exit could execute.* 31% of users split the difference.

Concept: The art of reading the gap between what financial statements say and what they actually mean, spotting manipulation before the market does.

Profile dimensions seen: Value Signal (momentum to fundamentals), Risk Appetite (cautious to bold). The App Store listing says there are ten.

## How the case is built

Structure is fixed and small. Every case is: hook video, one self-report question, one evidence card, one decision with three options, per-option analysis plus crowd percentage, four stats, outcome video, one-sentence concept, one quote, one lesson paragraph, two profile deltas, rating sheet.

The three decision options always map to the same shape: act hard, do nothing, split the difference. The split-the-difference option is the trap in this case, and the analysis explains why hedging fails when the structure is rotten rather than volatile.

The story is told twice. Video 1 sets up the tension and stops at the fork. Video 2 resolves it. The text screens between them are the mechanical part; the videos carry the emotion.

Everything is one screen, one idea. No screen holds more than one card.

## What to steal for the clone

1. The exact shape. Our step feed already has brief, decision, debrief. Add the self-report question before the brief and the concept card, quote card, and profile delta after the debrief. That is four new step types, each one card.
2. Per-option analysis with a crowd percentage. "31% of users split the difference" is one number and makes every pick feel measured. We can seed it from our own play data or a fixed estimate until real counts exist.
3. Profile deltas after each case: "+2 Fundamentals" on a named slider with a definition sentence. Two dimensions per case, ten total. This is the retention mechanic. The web funnel radar is the same data drawn differently.
4. Two-part narration. Write the story as a script that stops at the fork and resumes after the pick. Text or text-to-speech audio only. Edward's call: no AI video, the cost and time are not worth it for us.
5. "The data on your desk" as the name for the evidence step, with an IMPORTANT tag on the card that matters.
6. The three-option shape: act, hold, hedge. Make the hedge the trap sometimes so players learn that splitting the difference is a choice with its own failure mode.
7. The rating sheet chips. Cheap way to learn which cases work.
8. The "You chose" beat: a full-screen restatement before the reveal. Costs nothing and makes the decision feel weighty.

## What not to steal

- AI-generated video. Uncanny cartoon SBF, burned-in captions, roughly a minute per video. Edward ruled it out on cost and time.
- Numbers presented as fact without a source. "Under 30 cents on the dollar (projected)" was wrong by the time of the case; FTX creditors were being repaid at 118% or more in 2025. Our cases cite sources; keep doing that.

## Unverified

- How many profile dimensions exist and whether they persist across days without Pro.
- Whether the video is generated per case or a stock template.
