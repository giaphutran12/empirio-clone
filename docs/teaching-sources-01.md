# Teaching lessons 01–10: source audit

Checked 2026-09-20. This pack adds authored teaching content to the existing cases. Original case files, evidence IDs, research IDs, and option IDs are unchanged.

## Boundaries

- Choice scores are authored comparisons of the available actions, in five-point steps. They measure the fit of the offered move to the case, not writing skill, a probability of success, or agreement with history. Several defensible moves receive similar or equal scores.
- Baseline choice explanations use the original briefing, initial evidence, and option descriptions. They do not require a locked research finding or the halfway event. Research notes explain why an investigation matters without adding new results.
- Event changes apply only after the existing authored halfway event becomes visible. They change an option's timing, scope, promise, or required work while keeping its ID. These events and their effect on scores are authored teaching judgments, not historical claims.
- Practice questions introduce explicit hypothetical changes. Their answers teach how to respond to that new condition; they are not new facts about the real company.
- Only `history` contains later results. `connection` is the authored lesson drawn from those results, with limits on what the evidence can prove.
- All URLs below were opened with the web tool. Relevant text was inspected through the returned content, search excerpts from the same primary page, or find results. A failed Intel PDF fetch was not used; the official timeline supplied the stated follow-through instead.
- Company announcements and shareholder reports are primary company accounts, not independent causal studies. Later growth does not establish that the chosen plan was uniquely best, that every customer benefited, or that an alternative would have failed.

## 01: Coca-Cola, 1985

**Source:** [Coca-Cola's retrospective](https://www.coca-colacompany.com/about-us/history/new-coke-the-most-memorable-marketing-blunder-ever).

**Verified follow-through:** The original formula returned on July 11, 1985, 79 days after the change. Classic and the new formula were then sold alongside one another. The source also describes consumer protests and distinguishes taste preference from attachment to the original product.

**Teaching judgment:** A small sales trial scores well because it tests buying without removing the original. This is a predecision risk argument, not a claim that the trial would certainly have prevented the backlash. The production update changes “alongside” into a delayed national launch; it does not forbid the separate regional pilot.

## 02: Blockbuster and Netflix, 2000

**Decision source:** [Marc Randolph's firsthand interview, February 2021](https://tim.blog/2021/02/01/marc-randolph-transcript/). The $50 million offer and its rejection are attributed to his later account, not presented as a signed offer document.

**Follow-through source:** [Netflix 2003 annual report](https://s22.q4cdn.com/959853165/files/doc_financials/annual_reports/2003AR_print.pdf), shareholder letter on PDF page 5. Netflix reports its first profitable year, $6.5 million net income, and nearly 1.5 million members.

**Teaching judgment:** A checked purchase and a negotiated partnership both receive strong marks. The later independent business does not establish that an acquisition would have succeeded inside Blockbuster. The event requires any purchase to run separately at first; it does not invent an agreed partnership or a seller concession.

## 03: Intel, 1985

**Source:** [Intel corporate history](https://timeline.intel.com/), entries for 1985, 1986, and 1987. The 1985 entry describes stopping DRAM production. The 1986 entry describes its toughest economic year. The 1987 entry attributes a return to profit to a period that also included industry recovery and 386 success.

**Teaching judgment:** A staged exit and keeping two workable programs are both defensible before the resource constraint appears. The event makes two full programs unavailable, so “balance” must reduce scope or stretch schedules. No product margin or savings figure is invented. The historical connection explicitly avoids treating the exit as the sole cause of recovery.

**Fetch limitation:** The existing `history-1986-annual-report.pdf` URL returned a web-tool error in this pass. No new fact relies on that fetch.

## 04: Apple iPhone, January 2007

**Decision source:** [Apple's January 9, 2007 announcement](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2007/01/09Apple-Reinvents-the-Phone-with-iPhone/). It identifies Cingular as the exclusive US carrier, with over 58 million subscribers, and describes the planned rollout. The existing carrier research already contains that subscriber figure, so this pack adds no extra predecision fact.

**Follow-through source:** [Apple's September 10, 2007 release](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2007/09/10Apple-Sells-One-Millionth-iPhone/). It reports the millionth sale 74 days after the June 29 launch.

**Teaching judgment:** Launch, wider carrier talks, and a paid pilot each score 80 before the event. The case does not establish a unique best route. Wider reach is a legitimate aim; a missing essay is no reason to score it 10. Once store training visibly depends on a firm date, wider talks score 70 and explicitly accept later training. No unseen training deadline enters the baseline score.

**Limit:** Sales show demand through the chosen route, not profit or proof that exclusivity beat every possible wider deal. The authored training constraint is not claimed to be a documented historical carrier demand.

## 05: Apple iPod, October 2001

**Decision source:** [Apple's October 23, 2001 announcement](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2001/10/23Apple-Presents-iPod/), covering the $399 offer and Mac music-copying flow.

**Follow-through source:** [Apple's April 9, 2007 release](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2007/04/09100-Million-iPods-Sold/). It reports the 100 millionth sale after five and a half years and more than ten introduced models.

**Teaching judgment:** Premium and computer-bundle offers fit a clear initial buyer group. The later total is explicitly a product-family result, not validation of the original $399 price or Mac-only scope for everyone. The event commits the only available store display to easy music use; it does not invent a price test.

## 06: Adobe, spring 2013

**Decision source:** [Adobe 2013 annual report](https://www.adobe.com/content/dam/cc/en/investor-relations/pdfs/ADBE-10K-FY13-FINAL.pdf), PDF page 39, describes the May 2013 decision to put new creative features in Creative Cloud while continuing to sell CS6 licenses.

**Follow-through source:** [Adobe 2014 annual report](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/assets/investor-relations/pdfs/adbe-10k-fy14-final.pdf), PDF pages 40–41, reports 3.454 million paid Creative Cloud subscriptions at fiscal 2014 end versus 1.439 million at fiscal 2013 end.

**Teaching judgment:** A broader move and a phased move both score 80. Growth supports adoption, not universal buyer satisfaction or a proven reason for renewal. The event makes a clear answer about old-file access a condition before selling the changed offer to the concerned client. The lesson does not promise a specific legal or technical access policy.

## 07: Microsoft .NET, 2014

**Decision source:** [Microsoft's November 12, 2014 announcement](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2014/11/12/microsoft-takes-net-open-source-and-cross-platform-adds-new-development-capabilities-with-visual-studio-2015-net-2015-and-visual-studio-online/), describing opening the server stack and extending it beyond Windows.

**Follow-through source:** [The .NET team's June 27, 2016 release post](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/dotnet/announcing-net-core-1-0/). It records .NET Core 1.0 availability on Windows, macOS, and Linux, plus Microsoft support and a defined release support lifecycle.

**Teaching judgment:** The outcome is shipped, supported software, not a claimed revenue gain caused by open source. The authored user condition changes the open option to assign a support owner and a first supported scope before seeking adoption. It does not assume volunteers will carry Microsoft's support duty.

## 08: Amazon Kindle, November 2007

**Decision source:** [Amazon's November 19, 2007 launch release](https://press.aboutamazon.com/2007/11/introducing-amazon-kindle), for the $399 device, wireless book delivery, and store.

**Follow-through source:** [Amazon's May 19, 2011 sales release](https://press.aboutamazon.com/2011/5/amazon-com-now-selling-more-kindle-books-than-print-books). Since April 1, Amazon.com had sold 105 Kindle books for every 100 print books. The release excludes free Kindle books and includes hardcover and paperback sales even where there is no Kindle edition.

**Teaching judgment:** This is a unit-sales comparison on Amazon.com, not the whole book market, Kindle device sales, or profitability. The offer and prices evolved. The event removes a delayed title from the launch promise while leaving launch possible; the case does not establish that the missing title makes the whole catalog unusable.

## 09: Amazon Prime, February 2005

**Decision source:** [Amazon's February 2, 2005 results and Prime release](https://press.aboutamazon.com/2005/2/amazon-com-announces-record-free-cash-flow-fueled-by-lower-prices-and-free-shipping-introduces-new-express-shipping-program-amazon-prime), for the $79 fee and unlimited two-day shipping without an order minimum.

**Follow-through source:** [Amazon's 2017 shareholder letter, released in 2018](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/2017-letter-to-shareholders). Its Prime section reports more than 100 million paid members globally thirteen years after launch and describes expanded reach and benefits. The lesson attributes the number to the letter, not to a December 31, 2017 measurement.

**Teaching judgment:** Membership is evidence of adoption, not a stand-alone shipping profit calculation. The event turns full freedom into an explicit acceptance of separate ready-item shipments, removing any reliance on grouped boxes to make the plan work.

## 10: Airbnb, May 2020

**Decision source:** [Brian Chesky's May 5, 2020 staff message](https://news.airbnb.com/a-message-from-co-founder-and-ceo-brian-chesky), for nearly 1,900 departing staff, a narrower business scope, and staff support.

**Follow-through source:** [Airbnb's February 15, 2022 results release](https://news.airbnb.com/airbnb-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2021-financial-results), reporting full-year 2021 revenue 25% above 2019. The release also discusses recovering bookings and changing travel patterns.

**Teaching judgment:** This is a recovery after the cuts, not evidence that cuts alone caused it or that each cut was necessary. Temporary relief remains a reasonable choice under uncertainty. The event changes the focused-cut plan to require a shared staff message about work, timing, and support before executing it.
