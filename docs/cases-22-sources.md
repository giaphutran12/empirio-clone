# Cases 22–30 source audit

Authored and primary pages opened on 2026-09-20. Source IDs below are scoped to each case. Full source titles and URLs live in each case's server-only reveal. No company names, source URLs, later outcomes, or distinctive leaders appear in player evidence.

These are decision reconstructions, not transcripts of board meetings. Announcement-day cases place the player before the announced choice, with existing facts and proposed deal terms available. A proposal is never presented as an accomplished result. Later sources are used only for facts explicitly describing the earlier period, or for the reveal. No later performance result enters research unlocks.

The `halfway` item in every case is an authored three-hour event with `kind: simulation` and no source IDs. It creates a decision pressure, not a claim that the real company received that message. Research costs 1, 2, 2, and 3 hours are game design. Options, conditional consequences, lessons, interpretations, and rubrics are authored analysis. Unknowns mean the case packet lacks the item, not that real managers lacked it.

## 22: Johnson & Johnson / Tylenol

Decision cutoff: early October 1982, after the Chicago deaths and before the full recall response is settled.

- `deaths`, `poison`: s1, FBI Chicago history, 1982 section. Seven deaths and cyanide in capsules. The FBI's compressed September 29–30 account and the court's September 29–October 1 account differ slightly in dating, so the case uses early October and does not claim a precise final-death date.
- `product`, `strength`, `reach`: s2, FDA drug-history exhibit, Tylenol section. Non-prescription pain use, 1975 extra-strength marketing, 500 mg dose, and the historical sales comparison. The sales comparison retains FDA's qualified language.
- `insurance`: s3, federal district court opinion, scope-of-coverage discussion. Recall insurance had lapsed and internal records treated recall as self-insured. This is a pre-existing funding fact documented later, not a prediction of the court result.
- Reveal: s3 records broad withdrawal and new packaging work; s4 dates FDA packaging rules to 1982. No unsupported 31-million-bottle claim or claimed future market recovery is used.
- Event: a store group asks for one recall rule. Authored; not a historical retailer statement.

## 23: Domino's recipe

Decision cutoff: December 2009, before executing the US recipe launch.

- All facts use s1, Domino's December 16, 2009 announcement: `recipe` changes crust/sauce/cheese; `network` and `owners` describe the existing store system; `doubt` describes negative consumer perceptions.
- `testing`, `test-work`: the marketing chief describes two years of prelaunch work, frequent and lapsed users, cheeses, 15 sauces, and nearly 50 crust blends. No independent validation or repeat-purchase result is asserted.
- `deal`, `guarantee`: proposed introductory offer and satisfaction guarantee. These are launch terms, not realized sales or refund rates.
- Reveal reports the announced recipe, documentary-style marketing, and guarantee. It makes no sales-lift claim.
- Event: store owners request a stock/training/refund rule. Authored.

## 24: Chipotle food safety

Decision cutoff: November 20, 2015. s1 is an archived CDC page that contains several dated updates. Only November 20 or earlier sections supply player facts.

- `spread`, `hospital`, `missing-food`, `interviews`, `dna`, `trace`: November 20 update. 45 confirmed outbreak cases, six states, 16 hospitalizations, no deaths; 43 of 45 interviewed ate at the chain; ten genetic samples closely related; ingredient tracing underway and no precise food identified.
- `reopen`, `food-tests`: November 12 update. November 11 reopening began after agreed controls; food testing had not identified E. coli, with further results pending. The date is kept in the unlock so this is not misread as a current all-clear.
- Reveal only: s2 January 19, 2016 company release supports new testing, preparation, and training. The final CDC section supports no specific food found.
- Event: staff ask for a safety message. Authored. No new illness count is invented.

## 25: UPS holiday capacity

Decision cutoff: October 2013, before the holiday peak.

- All player facts use s1, a UPS-issued release hosted by PR Newswire, October 25, 2013. This is primary corporate material, not a reporter's summary.
- `days`, `growth`, `pickup`, `hiring`: 26 shopping days, six fewer, 8% forecast growth, over 34 million expected pickups December 16, planned 55,000 seasonal US hires.
- `delivery`, `repeated`, `monday`: expected deliveries over 29 million December 17, five delivery days above 2012 peak, over 32 million expected December 2 pickups and 10% expected increase. Forecast status is explicit; pickup and delivery metrics are kept separate.
- Reveal only: s2 fourth-quarter release describes overload, weather, delays, and actual 85,000 temporary staff. No realized peak is leaked into the October packet.
- Event: a large retailer asks for a late cutoff without firm volume. Authored.

## 26: Target Canada

Decision cutoff: January 2015, at the conclusion of the review but before approving the exit.

- `stores`, `staff`, `profit`: s1 January 15 company announcement, existing footprint, workforce, and management forecast. Profitability timing is a management assessment, not certainty about an alternative future.
- `cash`, `review`: s2 CEO Q&A, same day. Funding need and already-examined smaller footprints/distribution models.

## 29: Yahoo / Microsoft

## 30: Netflix / Qwikster

Decision cutoff: September 2011, before the separate DVD brand/site announcement. This is distinct from case 02's 2000 acquisition decision.

- s1 October 27 third-quarter filing is retrospective. `services`, `plans`, `combined`, `stream`, `disc-price`, `bluray` use its Domestic segment description and explicit account of the July pricing changes. No quarter-end subscriber count, churn rate, October action, or later content deal enters player evidence.
- `bluray` uses the filed $1–$4 surcharge for popular plans. The filing describes the service's pricing structure; it does not establish a day-specific September change. The case makes no such change claim.
- The proposed split is the decision premise. Research on friction and internal teams is explicitly interpretation, not a fabricated user study.
- Reveal only: the same filing's overview reports the branding/site reversal and separates the stronger price reaction from the lesser branding reaction. Copy does not claim the site split alone caused subscriber losses.
- Event: support asks who handles both-plan account problems. Authored.

## Validation boundary

The author checks data shape, source references, evidence ID uniqueness, four options with matching consequences, research budget, identity masking, and ordered research replay. Root integration owns catalog registration, API/build checks, computer-use receipts, and deployment. A source-backed packet is not proof that every real executive held the same information or that one option was historically inevitable.
