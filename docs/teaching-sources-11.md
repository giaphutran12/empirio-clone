## 011: Airbnb cereal, fall 2008

Source: [Polsky Center's firsthand Nathan Blecharczyk interview account, May 24, 2021](https://polsky.uchicago.edu/2021/05/24/fireside-chat-polsky-airbnb-cofounder-nathan-blecharczyk/).

The opening cereal passage records the 2008 sale and $30,000 used to reduce credit-card debt. The later funding passage dates the Y Combinator investment to January 2009. The history attributes the money figure to Blecharczyk; it does not treat cereal sales as proof of home-stay demand or as the sole reason Airbnb survived. Costs and paid orders remain unavailable during play.

The event changes `bridge`: samples for a reporter take time from host calls. Practice asks what changes if each box loses money. This hypothetical loss is not a historical claim.

## 012: Slack, early 2013

Sources: [John O'Farrell, June 20, 2019](https://a16z.com/announcement/slack/); [Slack's SEC-filed first-quarter fiscal 2020 earnings call](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1764925/000162828019007864/exhibit992-8xk.htm).

O'Farrell recounts Glitch's closure, the remaining cash/team and the new product entering the market in early 2014. The SEC-filed call reports more than 95,000 paid customers at April 30, 2019. These are paying customer organizations, not individual users. The history says customer groups. It does not equate customer count with profit or imply demand was already proved in 2013.

The larger prospectus at `/000162828019008125/slack424.htm` exceeded the browsing size limit. Its search snippet was not the final evidence; the smaller filed call was opened and inspected instead.

The event changes `test`: the willing outside team cannot attend a guided setup call. Practice separates repeat free use from an offer customers will pay for.

## 013: LEGO, early 2004

Source: [LEGO Annual Report 2005](https://www.lego.com/cdn/cs/aboutus/assets/blt6eacf5a8b7af1359/Annual_Report_2005_ENG.pdf).

Printed pp.11 and 27 plus Note 7 record the park-sale decision in 2004 and completion in July 2005. Printed p.25 and the group income statement on p.35 report net profit of DKK 505 million for 2005 against a 2004 loss of DKK 1,931 million, including discontinued activities. The same review attributes improved toy results to higher revenue and cost savings. The history does not call the entire net gain a result of selling parks, compare net profit with the opening operating-loss measure, or present a sale as cost-free.

The event changes `growth`: the shared team cannot launch a major theme and simplify the old range in the same season. Practice distinguishes park profit from cash timing.

## 014: Starbucks, early February 2008

Sources: [Craig Harris's on-site report, February 26, 2008](https://www.seattlepi.com/business/article/starbucks-gives-an-express-course-in-espresso-1265614.php); [Starbucks fiscal 2008 annual report](https://s203.q4cdn.com/326826266/files/doc_financials/SBUX2008ARv5_2008.pdf).

Harris observed staff training and reports nearly 7,100 company-operated US locations, excluding licensed shops. The annual report's fiscal 2008 highlights and selected financial data show consolidated comparable company-operated store sales down 3%; the US figure was down 5% and is not substituted for the worldwide figure. The report describes the economic slowdown and several other changes. No measured coffee-quality improvement, lost-evening revenue amount, or training-caused turnaround is asserted.

The archived Starbucks training memo PDF redirected to the generic history page when opened. Its indexed snippet is not used as final evidence. The contemporary firsthand report establishes the actual closure; the company report supplies the later result.

The event changes `rotate`: training during trade needs extra cover or more days. Practice introduces fresh checks showing only a few weak shops and available trainers, favoring focused help.

## 015: Patagonia, November 2011

Sources: [Patagonia's original jacket-ad explanation](https://www.patagonia.com/stories/planet/activism/dont-buy-this-jacket-black-friday-and-the-new-york-times/story-18615.html); [Our Quest for Circularity, March 11, 2021](https://eu.patagonia.com/no/en/stories/planet/our-footprint/our-quest-for-circularity/story-96496.html).

The original article supports the 2011 ad and its repair/reuse plan. The 2021 article's secondhand paragraph dates Worn Wear to 2013 and its online service to 2017, and reports over 130,000 repairs since launch. The history attributes this to Patagonia and dates the report. It does not make the count a present-day total, an audited waste reduction, or an effect caused by the ad.

The event changes `blunt`: the tighter layout makes repair information hard to read, so the ad must be shortened to show useful steps. Practice introduces a repair backlog that conflicts with a quick-help promise.

## 016: Nintendo, mid-2006

Source: [Nintendo Annual Report 2007](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ir/pdf/2007/annual0703e.pdf), operating results on PDF page 3.

The report identifies Wii's launch in 2006, 5.84 million worldwide console units sold in its first five months, and strong sales of both Wii Sports and The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess. The history uses this near-term result rather than later lifetime sales. Sales are not treated as repeat-use rates, proof of a specific buyer demographic or proof that equal launch space was necessary.

The event changes `both`: the retailer permits only one main demo while the other game remains available. Practice introduces first-use trouble among new players and asks for a simpler, retested demo.

## 017: Costco, March 2017

Sources: [Costco fiscal 2017 Form 10-K, Membership](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909832/000090983217000014/cost10k90317.htm); [quarter ended May 13, 2018, Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909832/000090983218000008/cost10q51318.htm).

The 10-K records the June 1, 2017 US/Canada fees of $60/$120 and the $1,000 Executive reward cap. The 2018 filing reports US/Canada renewals of 90% and approximately $109 million of revenue from the 2017 fee increases over the first 36 fiscal weeks. That revenue effect includes increases in certain other international operations, so the history says "its 2017 fee increases," not the US/Canada rise alone. Dollar amounts in the filing are millions. The 10-K explains that renewals are a trailing calculation, so a high rate does not establish that no members left after the rise.

The event changes `hold`: this notice batch retains the old fee and a later rise needs another notice round. Practice asks about top-tier downgrades hidden by steady total renewals.

## 018: IKEA, planning the 1956 range

Source: [IKEA Museum, Flatpacks](https://ikeamuseum.com/en/explore/the-story-of-ikea/flatpacks/).

The opening supports home assembly as part of the distribution model. The section "Planning starts at the drawing board" records missing parts, confusing instructions and corrective work during expansion in the 1960s and 1970s. These later problems appear only in history. No new profit estimate or claim that IKEA invented flatpacks is added. The history explains a real cost shifted into the customer's home rather than asserting that cheaper packing automatically creates a better product.

The event changes `expand`: use the older proven fitting while the new smaller one awaits a home test. Practice introduces a failed test, which is separate from the event's merely untested fitting.

## 019: Ford, early January 1914

Sources: [The Henry Ford's January 1914 applicant photograph and archival account](https://www.thehenryford.org/collections/explore/artifact/35765); [its $5 Day archival collection](https://www.thehenryford.org/collections/explore/sets/detail/henry-ford-5-day).

The photographic record and museum description support the applicant crowds, $5/eight-hour offer and intrusive qualification rules. The collection describes an original letter dated January 23, 1914, in which a worker's wife asks Ford to investigate her husband's treatment. The separate letter-detail URL timed out, so the source link is the accessible collection's dated archival description; no verbatim quotation or transcription is claimed. The letter is evidence that a complaint remained, not a population-wide measure of conditions. The history avoids inconsistent turnover percentages and does not rely on the 40-hour-week wording in a different museum page's summary.

The event changes `hire`: empty stations can take new hires, but those people still need coaching. Practice asks about injuries after pay attracts workers; that is a hypothetical condition, not an asserted historical injury trend.

## 020: Toyota, January 1984

Sources: Toyota's 75-year history, [NUMMI established](https://www.toyota-global.com/company/history_of_toyota/75years/text/leaping_forward_as_a_global_corporation/chapter1/section3/item2.html) and [NUMMI starts production](https://www.toyota-global.com/company/history_of_toyota/75years/text/leaping_forward_as_a_global_corporation/chapter1/section3/item2_a.html).

The second records 257 group/team leaders trained from mid-1984 to early 1985, the first Chevrolet Nova in December 1984, and the 1993 extension agreement. The history does not substitute later Corolla production for the first car, treat planned output as actual output, claim a measured quality gain, or imply the joint plant was the only viable route.

The event changes `join`: wider manager training requires explicit sharing limits. Practice changes the requested rights to use methods in all the partner's factories.
