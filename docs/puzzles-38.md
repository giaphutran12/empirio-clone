# Business puzzle arithmetic audit: 38–43

These six cases are authored scenarios, not accounts of named companies. Every input, research result and event is supplied by the case author. External historical claims and source URLs are intentionally absent. Opening grades use opening facts only. Locked research conclusions appear in research notes and post-decision teaching, not baseline assessment reasons.

Each case has four choices, three paid research tasks costing 2/2/3 hours, one visible halfway event changing a choice and its score, and a three-answer arithmetic check. Each table states its period and assumptions; uncertain target rows are labeled. Costs count the full stated operating bill, except case 43 deliberately uses cash flow including asset purchases rather than accounting profit.

## 38: Cloud Desk

One year and 100 continuously active customers: monthly sales 100 × 100 × 12 = 120,000; discounted annual sales 100 × 900 = 90,000; modest annual 108,000; full-price upfront 120,000. Costs 100 × 40 × 12 + 3,000 × 12 = 84,000 for each. Amounts left: 36,000 / 6,000 / 24,000 / 36,000. The optional-annual row explicitly assumes all choose it; a mixed cohort is not silently equated with that row. Churn research is conditional and not used in opening grades.

Event raises service cost by 10 per user per month: adds 12,000 to all rows. Cheap annual then loses 6,000. Opening fixed-cost break-even customer counts: 36,000 / (1,200 − 480) = 50 monthly; 36,000 / (900 − 480) = 85.714, so 86 annual. Practice: ten 120-dollar discounts give up 1,200 sales.

## 39: Market Lane

Current platform sales are fees: 1,000 × 100 × 10% = 10,000, not seller order volume 100,000. Current costs 1,000 × 5 + 3,000 = 8,000. Original campaign target gives 20,000 fees less 10,000 variable, 3,000 fixed and 6,000 ads = 1,000 left, versus 2,000 current. Higher fee at unchanged orders leaves 12,000 − 8,000 = 4,000. Platform-funded 10-dollar coupons add 10,000 costs; current orders then lose 8,000. No unmeasured coupon growth is assumed.

Event doubles ad quote to 12,000: target now loses 5,000. Extra orders contribute 5 each, so 6,000 campaign needs more than 1,200 extra orders to improve the current result. Locked fee-response scenario: 900 × 12 − 900 × 5 − 3,000 = 3,300. Practice: 200,000 × 10% = 20,000 fees.

## 40: Fit Box

All returned items have no resale value, so full stock purchase cost belongs in this comparison. Opening 100 orders and 40 refunds: net sales 10,000 − 4,000 = 6,000. Costs 5,000 stock + 1,000 outward shipping + 400 return handling + 1,000 fixed = 7,400, leaving −1,400. Unproved guide target at 20 returns: sales 8,000, costs 5,000 + 1,000 + 200 + 1,000 + 500 one-time guide = 7,700; leaves 300. Pausing orders still costs 1,000 fixed bills. The tighter-return option has no invented sales forecast.

Event adds 5 per return: 40 × 5 = 200 additional cost, leaving −1,600. Guide first-month opening-cost threshold: 22 returns leave 7,800 − 7,720 = 80; 23 leave 7,700 − 7,730 = −30. Practice: 30 full refunds leave 7,000 sales before costs.

## 41: Bench Works

Basic contribution per scarce machine hour = (80 − 40) / 1 = 40. Premium = (140 − 60) / 3 = 26.6667. All stated orders are firm for the week. Twenty premium use 60h, 2,800 sales, 1,200 direct + 1,000 fixed costs = 600 left. Thirty basic plus ten premium use 60h, 3,800 sales, 1,800 direct + 1,000 fixed = 1,000 left. Sixty basic give 4,800 − 2,400 − 1,000 = 1,400. Eighty basic with original overtime give 6,400 − 3,200 − 1,000 − 600 = 1,600.

Event changes overtime price to 1,000: leaves 1,200, which is 200 below normal basic production. Twenty extra basic items contribute 800, the overtime break-even price. A proposed fixture is unavailable and unproved, so no current capacity benefit is asserted. Practice: 90 / 3 = 30 per hour.

## 42: Bright Route

Eight hours of crew labor = 200 per day. Four scattered jobs give 400 − 200 − 40 supplies − 100 fixed = 60. Six dense jobs at 80 give 480 − 200 − 60 − 100 = 120. At 70 they give 420 − 360 = 60. Two scattered routes give 800 − 200 own crew − 300 agency crew − 80 added vehicle − 80 supplies − 100 fixed = 40. All return trips are included in travel budgets.

Event increases dense travel to four hours, leaving four one-hour jobs: 320 − 200 − 40 − 100 = −20. At five dense jobs, 400 − 200 − 50 − 100 = 50, below the current route’s 60; six are needed at this price. Practice independently asks that five-job total, answer 50.

## 43: Tool Shelf

Available capacity 8 × 30 = 240 tool-days. Current 80 days use one third. Current sales 80 × 30 = 2,400; costs 80 × 4 + 1,000 = 1,320; cash left 1,080. Original campaign target: 120 × 30 − 120 × 4 − 1,000 − 240 = 1,880. Price-cut target: 120 × 20 − 480 − 1,000 = 920. Buying eight assets without assumed new demand: 2,400 − 320 − 1,000 − 2,400 = −1,320 cash, not a claim of accounting loss. New assets remain owned; depreciation and resale are explicitly outside this exercise.

Event campaign price 900: 3,600 − 480 − 1,000 − 900 = 1,220, only 140 above current cash flow if its target arrives. Incremental days contribute 26 each; campaign break-even requires ceil(240 / 26) = 10 days originally, ceil(900 / 26) = 35 later. Locked short lease finding: eight extra days contribute 8 × 26 = 208 before 80 lease cost, leaving 128. Practice capacity: 60 / 240 = 25%.
